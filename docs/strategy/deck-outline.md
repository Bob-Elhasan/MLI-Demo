# Strategy Deck Outline

15 slides. One point per slide. The live demo follows slide 9 or closes the deck, depending on the room.

### 1. MLI Digital Ecosystem

- The one point: a website that earns trust and an app that sells and keeps policies.
- Supporting content: Misr Life and Lynkrs, date, "Strategy and demo". Placeholder wordmark, labelled as such.

### 2. What You Asked For

- The one point: two surfaces with two different jobs.
- Supporting content: the website (information, Investor Relations, intent capture, no buy button) and MLI Connect (the only digital purchase channel, plus track, pay, upsell and cross-sell).

### 3. Why Purchase Lives In The App

- The one point: the FRA requires facial recognition for digital sales, and the phone is where that check works.
- Supporting content: front camera, liveness, face match against the national ID. The website points every buyer to the app. The website never shows a premium.

### 4. Who We Are Designing For

- The one point: a digital-first person who will not call a broker or visit a branch, and may not be thinking about life insurance at all.
- Supporting content: the app converts demand, it does not create it. So the first product must feel small, fast and emotional. No invented personas or market stats on this slide.

### 5. The Website

- The one point: traditional, calm, trust-first, and every page routes to "Get MLI Connect".
- Supporting content: sitemap (Products, Investor Relations, About Us, Partners, Contact). Structure inspired by lemonade.com, not its look. Window-shopper capture: "Send Me The App Link" by WhatsApp or SMS.

### 6. Investor Relations

- The one point: one reliable home for disclosures, rebuilt on the current misrlife.com structure.
- Supporting content: Financial Reports, FRA And EGX Disclosures, Governance, Share Information, IR Contact. No marketing in the IR body. Listing status set by legal.

### 7. The Landing Page

- The one point: one page, one action, get the app.
- Supporting content: QR on desktop, store badges on mobile, how it works in three steps, product teasers, FAQ. The visual step up from the parent site without feeling like a different company.

### 8. MLI Connect: Buying In Minutes

- The one point: from download to covered in one sitting, with no branch and no paperwork.
- Supporting content: language, phone and OTP; ID scan, OCR review, selfie with liveness, Face unlock; three products (Simple Term Life, Maash Bokra, Family Protection Plan) with an indicative calculator; payday-aligned schedule, payment, e-signature, policy issued.

### 9. The Dead App Problem, Named Honestly

- The one point: this is a pulse app, not a habit app, and we design for that.
- Supporting content: nobody opens a life insurance app every day. Instead of chasing daily use, the app shows up on time at the few moments that matter.

### 10. The Cadence

- The one point: every screen maps to a rhythm.
- Supporting content: daily or weekly almost nothing (goals tracker only); monthly installment, the reliable pulse; every 6 months life event check-in, the cross-sell engine; yearly renewal, annual statement, beneficiary review; once, claims, where trust is made or lost.

### 11. The Monthly Pulse

- The one point: paying should take one tap and land after payday.
- Supporting content: payday-aligned date, saved method, Face unlock to approve, receipt, grace period with one-tap reinstatement, pause option. The app fixes lapse caused by forgetting and friction, not by affordability.

### 12. How The App Grows Policies

- The one point: cross-sell comes from life events the user tells us about, and from family.
- Supporting content: Life Event Check-In every 6 months (married, baby, new job) triggers a relevant card. Family Vault invites a beneficiary as a second user; "Protect Your Parents" turns that into a referral. Driven by the app's own data and declared events.

### 13. Claims And Trust

- The one point: claims is the one moment that decides whether the brand is trusted.
- Supporting content: intake form, document checklist and upload, status tracking with dates. Adjudication stays with the claims team. The app never promises a digital decision.

### 14. How We Will Measure It

- The one point: we measure kept policies and added policies, not app opens.
- Supporting content: on-time payment rate; persistency at month 13 and 25; policies per user; cross-sell conversion; cost per acquired policy versus the agent channel. Definitions from the journey map. No targets until we see MLI's baseline data.

### 15. What 20 Nov Is, And What Comes Next

- The one point: a full-fidelity demo on mocked backends, then a clear path to build.
- Supporting content: mocked vs real list. eKYC recommendation: FRA-approved local provider (Digified, Valify), first confirm what Dot IT already uses. Brand: transition palette, IBM Plex pair, logo pending. Decisions needed from Misr Life: date confirmation, product list and pricing logic, website premium position, Arabic legal review owner, logo files.
