# Marketing Council, Round 1

> Simulated council. Each take is built from the advisor's published frameworks and positions, not their actual review.

## The Question Before The Council

Does the MLI demo (website, /connect, MLI Connect app with all 11 flows) make Misr Life's leadership believe this ecosystem will sell policies and keep them paid? The stake is the 20 Nov demo: the client's confidence, and the scope that follows.

## Seated: Dunford, Sutherland, Hormozi, Handley, Sharp (Council Session)

Dunford for positioning against real alternatives, Sutherland for the behavior of buying and paying, Hormozi for funnel friction and offer value, Handley for the words. Sharp sits as the dissenter against the "differentiate and narrow" instinct.

---

### April Dunford, Positioning Against Real Alternatives

What would this customer do if MLI Connect did not exist? Not buy from a rival app. They would do nothing, or wait for an agent to turn up at a family gathering. The status quo is the competitor. The website hero, "Cover That Pays Since 1900", carries the one attribute no Egyptian insurtech can copy, and /connect pairs it with the phone. That pairing is the positioning: Egypt's oldest life insurer, bought in minutes on your phone. It shows up on /connect ("The Same Misr Life, Since 1900") but the app's own welcome screen does not say it.

The weaker point is in the app's sequence. A pitch walks the buyer through the perfect world before asking for anything. The app asks for a phone number, an OTP and a national ID scan before the user has seen a single product or price. That is the ask before the setup.

**Bottom line:** Lead with heritage plus phone everywhere, and show the products before you ask for identity.

### Rory Sutherland, Psycho-Logic Of Paying

The payday-aligned collection date, the on-time streak on the receipt and the pause option are the strongest ideas in the build, because they change how paying feels, not what it costs. That is the cheap psychological move his work argues committees undervalue. Keep them and make them louder in the pitch.

Two perception gaps remain. First, the identity scan before browsing feels like an interrogation before a shop has even opened its doors; the same scan after "I want this cover" feels like a security step protecting something the user now owns. Second, the receipt says "Payment Received", the language of the bank. The emotional truth of the moment is "your family is protected for another month", and the copy should say that. A secondary note: "I Already Have A Policy" leads into the new-customer flow, which tells an existing customer the company does not know them.

**Bottom line:** Move the scan to after desire, and reframe each payment as a month of protection bought.

### Alex Hormozi, Value Equation And Friction

Run the value equation on the first session. Dream outcome: a protected family. Perceived likelihood: decent, helped by 1900. Time delay: unknown to the user, because nothing says how long this takes. Effort and sacrifice: front-loaded, three verification steps before value. The denominator is doing all the damage. Show the price in the first minute and say "about 7 minutes to covered".

The calculator also undersells the dream outcome. "EGP 500,000 cover" is an abstract number. Translate it into a life the user recognises, such as years of their own income, and the same premium looks like a far bigger deal.

**Bottom line:** Value first, verification last, and make the cover number mean something.

### Ann Handley, The Words

So what? Because... The app copy is clean and mostly human, the Egyptian Arabic drafts sound like a person, and the claims screens are honest about who decides. The places that still sound like a company: "Payment Received", "Protection That Fits In Your Pocket" (a line that could sit on any insurer's app), and the trust facts that only appear on the website. Write to one person, Youssef paying on the 27th, and tell him what his money just did.

The Arabic headline "حماية على قد إيدك" needs a native ear: in Egyptian usage it can read as "affordable" or as "modest", and the two land very differently for a premium brand.

**Bottom line:** Every confirmation screen should tell one person what just happened to their family.

### Byron Sharp, Availability Over Differentiation (Dissenter)

The council is drifting toward "a remarkable app for digital natives who avoid agents". The evidence on brand growth says penetration wins and excluding buyers is self-harm. MLI already has physical availability: agents, bancassurance with NBE, the e& Misr partnership. An app positioned against those channels creates internal enemies and throws away reach. Let the app be the place everyone ends up, whoever brought them: give partners and advisors a way to refer into it and get credit.

He would also say the distinctive assets are not built yet. The placeholder wordmark is consistent across surfaces, which is good, but the brand needs one repeated device (the teal MLI tile, the 1900 line) used identically everywhere.

**Bottom line:** Make the app channel-inclusive and repeat a few distinctive assets relentlessly.

---

## Where The Council Disagrees

1. **Narrow digital-native target vs. channel-inclusive app.** Dunford and Hormozi sharpen the app for the person who will not talk to an agent; Sharp warns that excluding the agent and bank channels caps growth and makes enemies inside MLI. The real trade-off is focus vs. reach. What settles it: the client's distribution mix and how the sales force is paid. For the demo, an optional advisor or partner code satisfies both.
2. **Remove friction vs. friction as signal.** Hormozi wants every step cut; Sutherland's costly-signaling view says a visible identity check can increase trust at the moment of purchase. Both agree on timing: the scan belongs after the decision, where it reads as protection. What settles it: completion rate of browse-first vs. verify-first in a prototype test.
3. **Loud proof vs. honest gaps.** Hormozi wants hard proof (claims paid, ratings) up front; the build marks unverified facts "to be confirmed". Proof cannot be invented, so this stays open until MLI supplies verified numbers.

## Chair's Synthesis

The strategy holds. The pulse framing, payday collection, grace and reinstatement are the right bets. The build's biggest weakness is sequence, not substance.

### High
- **H1. Value before verification.** Browse products and see an indicative price before phone, OTP and eKYC. Verification starts at "Choose This Cover", and the price is re-confirmed from the ID age at summary.
- **H2. Real path for existing customers.** "I Already Have A Policy" goes to phone, OTP, "we found your policy", home.
- **H3. Fix the clipped "Branch / Phone" display words on the website home.**

### Medium
- **M1. Make the cover number mean something.** Show cover as years of the user's monthly income in the calculator.
- **M2. Channel-inclusive purchase.** Optional advisor or partner code at summary, credited to whoever referred the customer.
- **M3. Receipt speaks to the family.** "Your family is covered through {date}" alongside the receipt details.
- **M4. Trust at the moment of paying.** FRA licence and the 15-day cooling-off line on summary and payment.
- **M5. Say how long it takes.** "About 7 minutes from here to covered" on the welcome screen, and heritage plus phone in the welcome copy.

### Low
- **L1.** Native review of "حماية على قد إيدك" before the demo.
- **L2.** Replace "to be confirmed" facts with MLI-verified numbers as soon as they arrive.

**Tripwire:** Sharp's warning. If MLI's sales leadership is in the room, any copy that frames the app as replacing agents will cost the deal.

**Execute with:** copywriting for M3 to M5, cro for H1, marketing-psychology for the receipt framing.
