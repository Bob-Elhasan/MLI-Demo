# Marketing Council, Round 3

> Simulated council. Each take is built from the advisor's published frameworks and positions, not their actual review.

## The Question Before The Council

The product now holds together. On 20 Nov the demo itself is the sales presentation to MLI's leadership. Does it tell a story that changes their belief, and is it pointed at the right first market?

## Seated: Brunson, Halbert, Sutherland, Sharp (Council Session)

Brunson for the demo as a hook, story and offer, Halbert for which market to point it at first, Sutherland for the trust moments. Sharp sits as the dissenter against Halbert's "pick one hungry crowd".

---

### Russell Brunson, The Demo Is The Funnel

Apply hook, story, offer to the meeting itself. The hook is already strong: a 125-year-old insurer sold on a phone, with a live face scan in the room. The story is where demos usually die: a presenter clicking through 40 screens in whatever order they remember. His Perfect Webinar logic says find the one big domino, the single belief that makes every other objection fall. Here it is Bobby's own worry turned into the answer: "the app will not die after purchase, because it is built on the monthly payment pulse." Every beat of the demo should push that domino: buy in 7 minutes, then fast-forward through due, missed, reinstated, the 6-month check-in, and the rhythm screen as the close.

The presenter panel already has the jumps. What it lacks is the running order with the one line to say at each step, so the story survives nerves and rehearsals.

**Bottom line:** Build the run of show into the presenter panel, one beat per belief, ending on the pulse.

### Gary Halbert, The Starving Crowd

Before copy, before design: who is already hungry and already buying? The legacy prototype found them: Egyptians working abroad, who send money home every month, want their family protected in Egypt, and are used to doing money things on a phone. They have demonstrated buying behaviour (monthly transfers), dollar income, and a monthly ritual the payment pulse could ride. Maash Bokra USD already exists. His advice would be to make that crowd the sharp first wedge of the pitch.

**Bottom line:** Point the first campaign at Egyptians abroad, using the transfer they already make every month.

### Rory Sutherland, Trust Is Made When Things Go Wrong

The happy path proves the app works. Leadership will wonder what happens when a card is declined at 11pm on payday. A failed payment recovered in one tap (switch to InstaPay, done) builds more trust than ten successful ones, because it shows the company anticipated the user's bad moment. The grace and reinstatement scenarios already do this for missed payments; the same thinking belongs in the payment screen itself.

**Bottom line:** Show one failure and its graceful recovery, live.

### Byron Sharp, Reach Over A Single Crowd (Dissenter)

Halbert's wedge is attractive, but category growth comes from reaching all category buyers, and most of MLI's future buyers are at home in Egypt, reached through the bank and agent networks they already have. A pitch that leads with expats risks MLI hearing "niche product". Use the expat crowd as one proof of demand, not the positioning.

**Bottom line:** Mention the expat wedge as evidence of demand, keep the positioning broad.

---

## Where The Council Disagrees

1. **Expat wedge vs. broad reach.** Halbert wants a hungry first crowd; Sharp wants the pitch to stay category-wide. The trade-off is speed of first traction vs. size of the eventual market. What settles it: MLI's data on how many current Maash Bokra USD holders there are and how they were acquired. This changes scope, so it is Bobby's call.
2. **How much failure to show.** Sutherland wants a live failure; a presenter's instinct is to never show something break. Settled by framing: it is a designed recovery, rehearsed, not a bug.

## Chair's Synthesis

### High
- **R3-H1. Run of show in the presenter panel.** Ten beats, each with a jump and the one line to say, building to the payment pulse.

### Medium (Bobby's decision or next phase)

- **R3-M2. Failed payment recovery.** A declined card recovered in one tap via another saved method. Planned for the 2 to 10 Nov error-state pass.
