# Marketing Council, Round 2

> Simulated council. Each take is built from the advisor's published frameworks and positions, not their actual review.

## The Question Before The Council

After round 1 fixed the app's sequence, does the website and /connect carry a visitor from interest to the app without losing them, in the language they will actually read it in?

## Seated: Ogilvy, Schwartz, Hopkins, Godin (Council Session)

Ogilvy and Schwartz for research-led copy and awareness stage, Hopkins for specific, traceable claims. Godin sits as the dissenter against the direct-response bench, arguing for permission and remarkability over pressure.

---

### David Ogilvy, Research And The Specific Promise

He would start where his Gallup years taught him to: who is the reader, and what language do they read in? The client is Egyptian, the market is Egyptian, and the Arabic pages still switch to English mid-page: the chooser intro, the facts table, half the product details. A reader who meets English inside an Arabic page concludes the Arabic was an afterthought. For a considered purchase like life insurance, long factual copy sells, so the Maash Bokra page's key terms and early-withdrawal table are exactly right. They simply have to exist in Arabic.

**Bottom line:** Finish the Arabic on every page a client will be shown, before polishing anything else.

### Eugene Schwartz, Awareness Stage And Channeled Desire

Most Egyptians who land here are problem-aware at best: they know families get hit when a breadwinner dies, they have not shopped for cover. The home page enters correctly through life situations (the "What do you want to protect?" chooser) rather than the product. The break comes after the choice. A visitor who picked Maash Bokra clicks "Get MLI Connect" and arrives at a generic landing page and a generic app. The desire they just expressed is dropped on the floor.

**Bottom line:** Carry the chosen product from the website through /connect into the app's calculator.

### Claude Hopkins, Reason-Why And The Preemptive Claim

Generalities roll off the reader; specifics stick. The build already contains a specific, preemptive claim no competitor makes: premiums are collected two days after payday, by design. Every insurer could do it, only this one says it. Right now it is buried in the app's summary screen. It belongs on /connect as a headline-level reason to choose MLI Connect, stated plainly.

He would also flag a contradiction: /connect's "how it works" says the ID check comes first, while the app now lets people choose cover first. A salesman who says one thing and does another loses the sale.

**Bottom line:** Lead with the payday claim, and make the steps on /connect match the app exactly.

### Seth Godin, Permission Over Pressure (Dissenter)

The direct-response bench wants every page to push toward the app. His counterweight: the "Send Me The App Link" form is the most respectful thing on the site, because it lets someone who is not ready say "later" and still be welcomed back, and it promises one message only. Keep that promise visible and do not add pressure tactics on top. On remarkability he agrees with Hopkins from the other side: the payday collection is the thing people would tell a friend about. A product worth talking about is the marketing.

**Bottom line:** Make the payday idea the remarkable thing, and keep the app link a respectful, one-message permission.

---

## Where The Council Disagrees

1. **Push vs. permission on product pages.** Hopkins and Schwartz want each product page to drive straight into the app with the product preselected; Godin wants the "not now" path equally visible. The trade-off is conversion rate vs. trust. What settles it: both can coexist, a product-specific primary action plus the existing "Send Me The App Link" form.
2. **Repeated "Cairo, Our Home Since 1900" band on many pages.** Ogilvy would cut repetition that adds no new information; Sharp's round 1 view says repeated distinctive assets build memory. Left as is for now; revisit with client feedback.

## Chair's Synthesis

### High
- **R2-H1. Arabic completeness on the demo path.** Home, /connect, products index, product pages and the lineup carry no English inside Arabic pages.
- **R2-H2. Carry product intent.** Product pages link to /connect with the product; /connect names it and its QR and demo link open that product's calculator in the app.

### Medium
- **R2-M1. The payday claim, stated plainly on /connect.** "We collect your premium two days after payday" as a headline-level reason.
- **R2-M2. /connect steps match the app.** Choose your cover, then verify your ID, then pay and sign.

### Low
- **R2-L1.** Arabic for About, Partners, IR and forms, before the 11 to 17 Nov language pass.
- **R2-L2.** Revisit the repeated Cairo picture band after client feedback.

**Tripwire:** Godin's warning. If any change adds urgency tricks or extra messages beyond the one link, it costs trust that an insurer cannot buy back.
